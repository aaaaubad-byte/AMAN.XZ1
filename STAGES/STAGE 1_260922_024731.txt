The reference document explicitly establishes, among other things, the following architecture and security principles that must guide this project:

• Users/customers own their own customer numbers, protection requests, protections, and notifications.

• A customer can own multiple phone numbers.

• A phone number belongs to one customer.

• A phone number must not be duplicated.

• The telecom provider is determined automatically from the number prefix.

• The customer must not manually choose the telecom provider for a number.

• Protection plans belong to telecom providers.

• A protection request belongs to the customer, number, telecom provider, plan, and payment method as defined by the specification.

• A customer cannot directly create a protection.

• Protection creation must occur through the approved business flow.

• Overlapping active protections for the same number must be prevented.

• Historical protection data must preserve the relevant values from the time the protection was created.

• Changing plans must not modify historical protections.

• Changing task settings must not modify previously created tasks.

• "Needs renewal" is a calculated/display state, not an independently stored protection state.

• A customer must not be able to access another customer's data.

• Sensitive operations require secure backend/database logic and audit logging.

• Required secure operations include provider identification, protection-request validation/creation, request acceptance, request rejection, protection creation, task completion, task rescheduling, task cancellation, next-task creation, displayed protection-state calculation, and sensitive-operation logging.

• Administrative capabilities are protected operations and must not be implemented merely by hiding UI controls.

• The logical database includes entities such as users, customer_numbers, telecom_providers, protection_plans, payment_methods, protection_requests, protections, payment_tasks, notifications, audit_logs, system_settings, and task_settings as defined by the reference.

• System settings contain general application settings, while task settings belong to task_settings.

Do not implement business logic that belongs to later stages merely to make the project appear complete.
